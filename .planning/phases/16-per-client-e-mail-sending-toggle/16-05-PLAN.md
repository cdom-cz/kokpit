<objective>
Apply the switch to the payment confirmation, the second document e-mail of the system (it carries the cash receipt): a payment of a hand-served client is recorded as always, but its confirmation row is written as `skipped` with no job, the record dialog says so, and a confirmation still waiting in its grace window when the switch goes off is skipped by its job. The receipt of such a payment stays open, which is what the manual lock of 16-04 closes.

Purpose: P16-04 (skipped row, no job, payment never blocked), P16-05 (confirmation half: re-check after the claim, skipped, no lock, no revive), P16-06 (confirmation half: skipped is not a failure); D-06, D-07, D-08.
Output: one migration, the queue and job changes, the dialog hint, tests.
</objective>

- R-16-05-2 (queue): in `QueuePaymentConfirmation::handle()`, `$enabled = ClientDocumentEmails::allows($client)` on the client this method already reads fresh in the payment transaction (`clientOf()`); the row state is `Skipped` when the recipient is null or `$enabled` is false, else `Queued`; the cleaned recipient is stored either way; `SendPaymentConfirmation` is dispatched only when the recipient is not null and `$enabled` is true. Nothing else in the payment transaction changes. The class docblock gains a paragraph (Phase 16, D-06: a client with document e-mails switched off gets a skipped row with its address kept and no job; the payment is never blocked). `recipientFor()` is unchanged ("the address the confirmation would go to").

The `#[Idempotent(how: ...)]` text gains "a claimed row of a client whose document e-mails are switched off ends skipped without a send". The class docblock extends the AS-10 sentence: a confirmation skipped because the client has no usable e-mail or because the client's switch is off leaves the receipt open; the Admin's manual lock (Phase 16) closes it.

The docblock keeps its rule: the hint uses the gate the queueing uses.
