Decisions carried in (locked): D-06 (the bank account is editable on an open document; it follows the currency and is only ever taken from the settings, `reload_bank`), D-13 (every change to an issued document after its first send audited with who, when, before and after; never-sent documents behave like drafts). Assumption A12 and threat T-10.1-18 of plan 10.1-12: item texts and party lines may be in the Admin-only payload; the bank snapshot, the pay token and the internal note never are.

Owner decision (2026-10-23, fingerprint accepted by the owner, reason T-10.1-18): a change of the bank account is audited by a non-reversible keyed fingerprint, the fingerprint before and the fingerprint after, with who and when, and not by the account values; the account number never enters the history, on purpose. This narrows the "before and after" of D-13 and IN-15 for this one field only. Plan 10.1-16 records the ruling: it amends the IN-15 text in `.planning/REQUIREMENTS.md` and adds the ruling to D-13 of `10.1-CONTEXT.md`. This plan does not edit either file.

Design choice (Claude's discretion: names and wording are free in CONTEXT): the marker is keyed (HMAC with the application key) rather than a plain hash, because a Czech account number has a small search space and a plain truncated SHA-256 over a known JSON shape could be brute-forced back to the account; with the key it cannot, and an attacker who holds the application key already holds the database. 12 hex characters are enough to tell two accounts apart in a history row and short enough to read. The canonical form sorts keys recursively before encoding, because the row read from PostgreSQL jsonb and the array taken from the settings carry the same account in different key orders.
